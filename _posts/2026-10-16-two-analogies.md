I'm trying to get some opinion on the typical conversation that goes like this:

-- I don't want to use AI, nobody should use AI. 

-- You have no choice. Soon it will not make sense not to use it.

In this post I explore two analogies related to this situation, without any 
conclusion. 

Analogies can be misleading, and some might 
think reasoning should be abstract, not example-based. To me they are useful. 
In my mind it is similar to the following situation. If you tell me that I can 
get a coffee but 
there is a probability of 10^{-10} that it is poisoned, I will hesitate because I 
cannot refer to a similar situation; but if you tell me that this probability 
is much lower than the probability of being hit by a meteorite during my 
lifetime, then I will drink it. 

## Analogy 1: Computation by hand

Consider some computation that is done by hand, and can then be replaced 
by computers. For example, some approximately solving partial differential 
equations for dam safety. 

Imagine that we are at the moment where some people use computers for this 
task, and others don't. People who do not use computers might have several 
reasons for not doing so: they cannot afford a computer, they are against using 
computers made by unethical companies, they value the 
process of doing these computations and the community around it. 

The likely scenario here is that the community converges on using 
computers for this task. 
At some point, the reviewers
will consider that computation by hand has no value, because most
people in the community can do the same in a second on their computers. 
And for the remaining people doing computation by hand, then either they 
get a job in a museum that preserves this know-how, or change 
the way they present their research ("low-tech alternatives"?), or move on to 
doing something completely different. 

There is some period when computation by hand is still valued, 
either because the reviewing peers do not have computers yet, or 
because there is some inertia or a feeling of empathy. 

All the work that have been done without computers will have some weight on a CV 
for a few years, but this weight decreases with time.

## Analogy 2: Torturing monkeys

Consider a situation where a research group obtains groundbreaking results
of the highest technical quality, but tortured thousands of monkeys in the 
process (think about something even worse, if needed). 

How does the community adapt to this situation? Let's assume that almost 
everybody refuses to torture monkeys. 

Are people going to pretend this experiment never happened and they have never 
seen the results? 
I guess not. Even if people wanted to, they will hesitate 
to spend years of their lives trying to contradict something that everyone
secretly knows to be established. 
Maybe people would try to reproduce the results in a more acceptable way, 
in an effort to restart from a clean basis. 
And then the community as a whole would consider this to be the work to be praised, 
I guess?
